# Ep 1 Short — "Carbonara Isn't As Italian As You Think" (vertical, ~50s)
Narrator: Benji. Stand-alone (no "next time" teaser). All claims come from the fact-checked long script (`../script.md`).

## Narration
Carbonara is Italy's most sacred pasta. Eggs. Guanciale. Pecorino. Pepper. Never cream.
So it must be ancient, right? Not quite.
The earliest known mention of carbonara in print is from nineteen-fifty.
Six years earlier, in nineteen forty-four, Allied troops reached Rome — with supplies of bacon and powdered eggs.
Some food historians believe that's when Roman cooks created the dish.
That nineteen-fifty newspaper story? It's about American officers hunting for spaghetti alla carbonara in Rome.
The first known recipe was printed in Chicago, in nineteen fifty-two.
And for decades, many versions even used cream.
So is carbonara Italian, or American?
As one Italian food writer put it: Italian genius… American resources.
