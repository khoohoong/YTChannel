## 1. The big idea: the dish is the character
Every format in the PDF is told in the first person ("I was struggling with…"). We can't do that, and we shouldn't
fake it. Instead, swap the person for the **food**: carbonara "was born", "travelled", "changed", "got into fights".
Viewers follow a character with a journey far better than they follow a list of facts.

## 2. The backbone for every long episode: the Story Spine (Pixar)
Eight beats, linked by cause and effect. The key word is **"because of that"**: every beat must *cause* the
next one, and each one opens a new question that keeps people watching.

| Beat | Carbonara (what we actually made) |
|---|---|
| **Once upon a time…** | Roman countryside cooks tossed pasta with cured pork and pecorino (gricia) |
| **Every day…** | Eggs and cheese on pasta were nothing new in southern Italy (Cavalcanti, 1830s) |
| **Until one day…** | June 1944: Allied troops reach Rome, carrying bacon and powdered eggs |
| **Because of that…** | Roman cooks feeding hungry soldiers put the two together |
| **Because of that…** | 1950: a newspaper records American officers hunting for "spaghetti alla carbonara" |
| **Because of that…** | 1952: the first recipe is printed, in Chicago; then 1954 in Italy, with Gruyère and garlic |
| **Until finally…** | 1990s: cream is thrown out and the strict 4-ingredient version is locked in |
| **Ever since then…** | Rome defends it as ancient; in 2025 UNESCO honours Italian cuisine |

**What this tells us about Ep 1:** the strongest stretch (soldiers → newspaper → Chicago → cream) is a pure
"because of that" chain. The weakest stretch is the **three legends** (charcoal burners, secret society, pepper):
it's a *list*, not a chain, and nothing causes anything. **Rule for Ep 2 onward:** myths go in fast (one
line each, inside the hook or "Every day…" beat), and the body is always a causal chain.

### Long-episode template (8–10 min)
1. **Hook (0:00–0:20):** the myth everyone believes, plus the crack in it (see §5 hooks)
2. **Once upon a time / Every day:** the world before the dish (keep it to about 45s)
3. **Until one day:** the trigger event, with a year tag on screen
4. **Because of that × 3–5:** each beat = one place + one year + one piece of evidence
5. **Until finally:** the moment the dish becomes what we know
6. **Ever since then:** where it is today, plus the modern fight or myth (comment bait)
7. **Close:** one-line verdict that answers the hook. No next-episode teaser (episodes stand alone).

**Two "secret sauce" rules that carry over:**
- **Show receipts.** The PDF says proof must be visible. For us, every claim gets an on-screen object: the 1950
  newspaper, the 1952 Chicago guide, the 1954 magazine, with year tags. We already do this, so keep it.
- **Bigger gap = more watch time.** Pick topics where the myth and the truth are far apart (fortune cookies
  "from China" → Japan/California; croissant "French" → Vienna).

## 4. Shorts formats (30–55s), built from each long episode
Make **3 Shorts per episode**, each a different format, so one week's work fills the feed:
1. **The Myth-Buster** (what we made): hook = myth, 4–5 "because of that" beats, verdict. ~50s.
2. **Before & After**: same dish then vs now, side by side, one hard cut on a beat. ~20s, almost no narration.
3. **The Mentor Quote**: one real person, one vivid moment, their exact words on screen. ~30s.

## 5. Hooks: Kallaway's four S's, applied
Every first line must have: **Subject** (name the dish in sentence one), **Stakes** (why keep watching: a
surprise, a fight, a lie), **Simple** words (a 9-year-old understands), **Super clear** (short, active voice).

| | Hook | Subject | Stakes | Simple | Clear |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ep 1 long (current) | "Ask anyone in Rome, and they'll tell you: carbonara is sacred." | ✅ | ❌ stakes arrive at 0:30 | ✅ | ✅ |
| Ep 1 long (better) | "Carbonara is Italy's most sacred pasta. But there's no record of it before 1950." | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Ep 1 Short (current) | "Carbonara is Italy's most sacred pasta." (the on-screen text carries the stakes) | ✅ | 🟡 | ✅ | ✅ |
| Ep 1 Short (better) | "Carbonara isn't as Italian as you think. The proof is a 1950 newspaper." | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**Rule:** the spoken first line and the on-screen text should do *different* jobs. Say the subject, and show the stakes.

## 6. Kallaway's six growth patterns, applied to Origin Plate
1. **Visual moat (signature look).** We already have one most channels don't: clay stop-motion, clay-coloured
   maps, year tags. To strengthen it:
   - **Recurring characters.** Make *Nonna* (the clay grandmother) the channel's mascot and put her in every
     episode's hook and verdict, like a host who never speaks. Same for the young cook.
   - **Own typography.** Swap the default font for one signature typeface on all titles, tags and captions.
   - **Signature opener/closer.** A 2-second clay "Origin Plate" sting and the same end card every time.
2. **Delivery cadence.** Benji is a voice model, so cadence comes from how we write:
   - One idea per sentence, about 15 words maximum (Kallaway's "Pac-Man" point: the brain can only take so many words per breath).
   - Put the power word **last** ("The first recipe was printed… in Chicago.").
   - Use "…" for deliberate pauses before reveals. We did this on "Nothing." and it worked.
3. **Anti-slop protocol.** His warning: if AI does everything, every channel converges on the same look.
   Our defences (keep them):
   - **You** pick the topic and the angle.
   - **Every fact is checked** against live sources and logged. AI channels skip this.
   - Hand-built cue sheets and timing (not auto-edited).
   - **Go to the edges, not the crowded middle.** Few channels do *accurate* Southeast Asian food history
     (nasi lemak, laksa, chicken rice, satay). That's an under-served lane where you have a home advantage.
4. **Ideas = topic + angle + substance.**
   - Topic: pick from proven demand (dishes people search and argue about).
   - Angle: always "the story you were told vs what the evidence says".
   - Substance: dated receipts and named people.
   Before each episode, check how well that topic has done for other channels and only greenlight topics with
   proven demand.
5. **Volume while keeping taste.** Our pipeline is now fast (script → voice → clips → auto-assembly).
   Target: **1 long episode + 3 Shorts per week**. AI handles research, assets and assembly; you handle topic,
   angle and approvals, where taste matters.
6. **Four S's of hooks.** See §5. Every script gets a hook check before recording.

## 7. Checklist added to every script, before recording
- [ ] Hook passes the four S's (subject in line 1, stakes by line 2)
- [ ] Body is a "because of that" chain, with no list sections longer than 15s
- [ ] Every beat has a place, a year (on-screen tag) and a visible piece of evidence
- [ ] At least one named person with a direct quote (Mentor sauce)
- [ ] Sentences ≤ 15 words, power word last
- [ ] Ending answers the hook; no next-episode teaser
- [ ] 3 Shorts planned: Myth-Buster, Before & After, Mentor Quote
