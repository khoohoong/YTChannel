# SERIES PLAN — "THE VANISHED KINGDOMS" (Lost Empires of Southeast Asia)
### Option C expanded into a trilogy. Drafted 2026-07-05. Phase 0 — concept, no spend.

> **Series premise:** Three empires that each dominated Southeast Asia for centuries — and then
> disappeared so completely that the world forgot they existed. One was erased until a French
> scholar re-discovered it in 1918. One vanished off the map within the era of photography. One
> died — and was resurrected as the founding myth of the world's fourth-largest country.
>
> **Series arc:** WATER → STONE → MYTH. An empire lost to the sea, a kingdom lost to conquest,
> an empire reborn as a nation. Each episode is standalone-safe (Bright Side rule: every video
> must work cold from search/recommendation), but escalates a shared question: *how does an
> entire civilization disappear?*

---

## EPISODE 2 — SRIVIJAYA: "The Empire That Vanished for 600 Years"
**Era:** ~7th century – 13th/14th century · **Target runtime:** ~8–9 min (measure VO, don't trust estimates)

**The hook (cold open):** Right now, in Indonesia, fishermen are diving into a muddy brown river
— and coming up with golden rings, temple bells, and sword hilts. They're pulling up the remains
of an empire that dominated all of Southeast Asia for six hundred years… an empire so completely
erased that until 1918, nobody on Earth knew it had ever existed.

**Segment outline (Phase 0 — fact-check before full script):**
1. **HOOK** — the Musi River divers + "the empire nobody knew existed."
2. **THE CHOKE POINT** — why the Malacca Strait made whoever controlled it rich; Srivijaya as
   the toll-keeper of east–west trade (China ↔ India ↔ Arabia). *(map renderer: trade routes)*
3. **THE FLOATING EMPIRE** — a capital of wood and water at Palembang; a navy instead of walls;
   a world center of Buddhist scholarship ("Island of Gold"). *(re-hook: an empire of wood has
   one fatal weakness — it can rot away without leaving a single ruin.)*
4. **THE ENEMY FROM THE WEST** — 1025: Rajendra I of the Chola dynasty (south India) launches a
   naval raid across the Bay of Bengal, sacks the empire's ports, breaks its monopoly.
5. **THE VANISHING** — slow fade: trade shifts, rivers silt, rivals rise (Majapahit), the wooden
   city dissolves back into the river. No stone temples like Angkor = nothing to find.
6. **THE REDISCOVERY** — 1918: George Cœdès assembles scattered inscriptions and proves a lost
   empire connected them; 2011–present: the river starts giving the gold back. **CTA:** "an
   empire the size of Western Europe's trade network left almost no trace. What else have we
   lost?" → tees up Episode 3.

**Why the "sparse visuals" worry is solved:** underwater treasure, river divers, wooden stilt
cities, monsoon war fleets, burning ports — all photoreal Kling/Seedance territory; plus 3–4
code-rendered route/empire maps. It's *more* visual than a temple tour.

**Title candidates:** "The Empire That VANISHED for 600 Years" · "They Found a Lost Empire at
the Bottom of a River" · "The Richest Empire You've Never Heard Of"
**Thumbnail:** hand rising from dark water holding a gold ring; text: "FORGOTTEN FOR 600 YEARS"

## EPISODE 3 — CHAMPA: "The 1,600-Year Kingdom That Fell Off the Map"
**Era:** 192 CE – 1832 · **Target runtime:** ~8–9 min

**The hook:** There was a country on the coast of Vietnam that existed for more than sixteen
hundred years. Older than Byzantium, and it outlived it. It fought off Angkor, and the Mongols.
And then, in 1832 — within the lifetime of people who saw photography invented — it was deleted
from the map so thoroughly that most of the world has never heard its name.

**Segment sketch:** (1) HOOK — the country that lasted 1,600+ years, gone. (2) Lords of the
coast — Cham sailors astride the South China Sea trade. (3) The tower builders — Mỹ Sơn's brick
temples (now UNESCO). (4) Wars of giants — Champa vs. Angkor, vs. the Mongols, vs. Đại Việt's
slow southward march. (5) 1832 — Emperor Minh Mạng annexes the last principality, Panduranga;
the last king arrested. (6) The survivors — the Cham people are still there today, a living
minority; the kingdom died, the people didn't. **CTA** tees the finale: some empires vanish…
and some come back from the dead.
*(Tone note: living descendants — handle with respect, per playbook review gate.)*

**Title candidates:** "The 1,600-Year-Old Country That Vanished" · "This Kingdom Outlived Rome —
Then Disappeared"
**Thumbnail:** Mỹ Sơn tower half-swallowed by jungle; text: "GONE IN 1832"

## EPISODE 4 — MAJAPAHIT: "The Dead Empire That Built a Modern Nation"
**Era:** 1293 – ~1527, afterlife → today · **Target runtime:** ~8–9 min

**The hook:** In 1293, Kublai Khan — ruler of the largest empire in history — sent a thousand
ships to punish one Javanese king. A local prince named Raden Wijaya did something nobody had
tried before: he *joined* the Mongols, used them to destroy his enemy, then turned on them and
threw them into the sea. On the ashes, he founded an empire. And when that empire died two
centuries later… it refused to stay dead.

**Segment sketch:** (1) HOOK — the prince who played the Mongols. (2) Gajah Mada — the prime
minister who swore the Palapa oath: no rest until the whole archipelago was united. (3) Golden
age — the Nagarakretagama's 98 tributaries, Sumatra to New Guinea. (4) The fall — succession
wars, rising Islamic sultanates, the court fades (~1527). (5) **The resurrection** — 400 years
later, Indonesian nationalists reach for Majapahit as proof the archipelago was once one;
Indonesia's first satellite (1976) is named **Palapa** after Gajah Mada's oath. A 14th-century
promise, launched into orbit. **Series close:** empires vanish three ways — into water, into
other nations, and into stories. Stories are the only ones that come back.

**Title candidates:** "The Empire That Came Back From the Dead" · "How a Dead Empire Built
Indonesia" · "He Tricked the Mongol Empire — and Built His Own"
**Thumbnail:** gold-lit royal silhouette over a satellite in orbit; text: "DEAD. THEN REBORN."

## RELEASE ORDER RATIONALE
Srivijaya opens (the user's pick + strongest pure-mystery hook + treasure-hunt cold open).
Champa deepens the theme (not just forgotten — deliberately erased). Majapahit closes on the
reversal (a vanished empire that *won* in the end) and lands in the present day — the strongest
possible series finale beat. Chronological order would put Champa (192 CE) first; not worth
trading away the treasure-diver cold open.
